# Sources

Every bundled melody is public domain in the US, or original. Only melodies are encoded, never recordings or later arrangements. Matthew checks each by ear.

| `st-james-infirmary.json`  | "St. James Infirmary" by "Joe Primrose" (Irving Mills), Gotham Music Service, © 1930. The first phrase of the refrain, from the scan on [IMSLP](<https://imslp.org/wiki/St._James%27_Infirmary_(Mills,_Irving)>). E minor, as printed. The original's cut time is written as 4/4 with the same rhythm, plus a one-beat pickup. The edition prints all three verses under one melody line. The rhythm follows the well-known third verse ("…Saw my baby there, / Stretched on a long white table, / So sweet, so cold, so bare"), which fits the printed notes one syllable per note, except in bar 2: there the E under "In-fir-ma-ry" is struck twice (eighth, then dotted half) so each syllable gets its own note. The syllable-by-syllable table is in `docs/overnight/st-james-verse3.md`. | Public domain in the US (published 1930; entered the public domain Jan 1, 2026). Not public domain in Canada. |
| `amazing-grace.json`       | "Amazing Grace" (tune New Britain), "E. O. E. Arr.", No. 235 in E. O. Excell, _Make His Praise Glorious_ (Chicago: E. O. Excell, 1900), [scan leaves 225–226](https://archive.org/details/makehispraiseglo00exce/page/n225/mode/1up) on the Internet Archive. The soprano line of the first verse, all 14 bars plus the pickup, in A-flat major and 3/4 as printed. Excell writes the end of each half as a half note with the next phrase's pickup in the same bar (no extra held bar), and that is kept.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Public domain (published 1900)                                                                                |
| `greensleeves.json`        | The traditional tune "Greensleeves" as set in "What Child is This?", No. XIV in John Stainer and H. R. The soprano line of the verse (8 bars plus an eighth-note pickup), in E minor and 6/8 as printed; the chorus is left out.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Public domain (published 1871)                                                                                |
| `when-the-saints.json`     | **No edition: the traditional melody as commonly sung.** Matthew chose this on Oct 7, as an explicit exception to the cite-an-edition rule. No print before 1931 that carries this tune was found: J. M. Black and K. E. Purvis's 1896 "When the Saints Are Marching In" is a different tune, and R. E. Winsett's 1927 gospel arrangement (_Voice of Glory_, No. 148; again in _Soul Inspiring Songs_, 1929, No. 183) has the refrain's words only as a bass echo under a different soprano line. The refrain is encoded in F major, 4/4, with a three-beat pickup, and checked against the Wikimedia Commons file [When The Saints.mid](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:When_The_Saints.mid) (in G, transposed down a whole step), not against a recording.                            | Traditional; pending Matthew's ear check                                                                      |
| `sweet-georgia-brown.json` | "Sweet Georgia Brown" by Ben Bernie, Maceo Pinkard and Kenneth Casey (New York and Detroit: Jerome H. Remick & Co.); the first music page reads "Copyright MCMXXV by Jerome H. Remick & Co." Read from the scans of a copy at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln's [History Harvest](https://historyharvest.unl.edu/items/show/183) (the same edition is catalogued in Mississippi State's Templeton collection and on IMSLP). The vocal line of the 32-bar chorus, pp. 4–5, with the 2nd ending, in G major as printed. The cut time is written as 4/4 with the same rhythm. Bars 15–16 have two notes marked "spoken ad lib"; they are left out, so bar 16 is a rest. Where the two verses under bars 25–30 tie notes differently, the first verse's attacks are kept.                        | Public domain in the US (published 1925; entered the public domain Jan 1, 2021)                               |
