# Ear check: the new demo songs

For Matthew. One section per tune: where it came from, what I chose, and what to listen for. Open each from the landing's song cards, press Play, and compare with the edition. Syllables are named by position only, never quoted. Provenance is in `content/songs/SOURCES.md`, and the reasoning behind each choice is in `docs/decisions/more-songs.md`.

## When the Saints Go Marching In (check this one first)

- **Source:** none printed. This is the traditional refrain as commonly sung, your exception to the edition rule (Oct 7). No pre-1931 print carries this tune: the 1896 Black/Purvis song is a different tune, and Winsett's 1927 gospel version has these words only as a bass echo. The only cross-check is the Wikimedia Commons MIDI "When The Saints.mid" (in G), transposed down a whole step. The encoding matches it note for note, so if it sounds wrong to you, trust your ear over the MIDI.
- **Key, meter, tempo:** F major, 4/4, quarter = 104. A three-beat pickup (F, A, B-flat), then 15 bars, ending on a whole-note F.
- **Judgment calls:**
  - Each call ("1 3 4" then a held 5) holds the C for three beats with a beat of rest, so the next pickup lands cleanly, as the MIDI has it.
  - Bar 8 (the start of the second half) is G, A, G after a rest.
  - Bar 11 is C (one beat), then B-flat (two beats), then a rest. This is the spot most likely to differ from how you sing it.
  - The last line is B-flat, A, B-flat into C, A, F, G, F.
- **Listen for:**
  - The 1-3-4-5 call, three times. On the third, 5 falls through 3 to 1 and stops on 2 (G), an open, waiting sound.
  - The second half, after a rest: 2-3-2, then 1 held, 1, then 3 up to 5, 5 down to 4. The last call is 4-3-4, 5, 3, 1, 2, 1.
  - The last bar should feel completely settled on F.

## Amazing Grace

- **Source:** E. O. Excell, _Make His Praise Glorious_ (Chicago, 1900), No. 235, "E. O. E. Arr.", on the Internet Archive ([leaf 225](https://archive.org/details/makehispraiseglo00exce/page/n225/mode/1up), continued on leaf 226). The soprano line of verse 1.
- **Key, meter, tempo:** A-flat major as printed, 3/4, a one-beat pickup, quarter = 80. Fourteen bars plus the pickup, ending on the tonic.
- **Judgment calls:**
  - Excell has no held bar at either phrase end. The high E-flat at the midpoint is a half note, and the second half's pickup (a dotted eighth and a sixteenth, C then E-flat) shares its bar. The last bar is a single half note. Both are kept as printed.
  - Bar 6 ends with two eighths, B-flat then A-flat (degrees 2 then 1), before the leap up to the high E-flat.
  - Bars 8 and 10 have Excell's dotted figures. Bar 8 is E-flat dotted quarter, C, E-flat, C; bar 10 is A-flat dotted quarter, B-flat, A-flat, F.
- **Listen for:**
  - A pickup on 5 below home (E-flat), then 1, 3-1, 3, 2.
  - The first half lands up on 5, an open, unfinished sound. The second half comes back down and closes on 1.
  - In 3/4 the long notes fall on the downbeats. If it sounds like it's in 2 or 4, the bar lines are wrong.

## Greensleeves

- **Source:** John Stainer and H. R. Bramley, _Christmas Carols New and Old_ (Novello, Ewer, [187-]), No. XIV, the carol set to "Greensleeves", p. 32 ([leaf 37](https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsn00staiiala/page/n37/mode/1up) on the Internet Archive). The soprano line of the verse.
- **Key, meter, tempo:** E minor as printed, 6/8, an eighth-note pickup, quarter = 84 (dotted quarter = 56, two lilting beats a bar). Eight bars plus the pickup. The chorus is left out.
- **Judgment calls:**
  - Stainer's bar 4 is F sharp (dotted quarter), then the low B (quarter), then E as the pickup into bar 5. (The D sharp printed with that F sharp is the alto, not the tune.)
  - C is natural in bars 1 and 5 (the climb to the top of the phrase), and C sharp appears only in bar 7, in the cadence figure (D sharp, C sharp, D sharp).
  - The last bar is two dotted-quarter E's, as printed, not one held note.
- **Listen for:**
  - Two big beats per bar, each split in three (long-short, or three even eighths). It should swing gently, not march.
  - The leading tone, D sharp, pulling up to E at the end of bars 3 and 7. The natural D in bars 2 and 6 sounds darker, older.
  - The ending: D sharp, C sharp, D sharp, then E, E. That's the minor-key home, and it should feel settled.
  - The staff should show the eighths beamed in threes. One flag per eighth means the 6/8 beaming fix regressed.
