# More demo songs: decisions

Stream: `feature/more-songs`. Matthew: "I think we definitely need more than two songs." Every tune comes from a cited public-domain edition, read note by note off the scan (the St. James lesson in `DECISIONS.md`), unless noted.

- **2026-10-07 — Amazing Grace is encoded from Excell's 1900 arrangement, as printed.** No. 235 in _Make His Praise Glorious_ (Chicago, 1900), which Hymnary.org credits as Excell's 1900 adaptation of the tune; the scan is on the Internet Archive. A-flat major, 3/4, a one-beat pickup, the whole first verse (14 bars). Excell's notation is kept: each half ends on a half note with the next pickup in the same bar, so there's no extra held bar, and bar 6 has two eighths (degrees 2 then 1) on its third beat. _Rejected:_ William Walker's _Southern Harmony_ (1835), the tune's first pairing with these words, which I didn't read (Excell's is the form people know); smoothing any bar toward a remembered version.
- **2026-10-07 — Greensleeves is encoded from Stainer's "What Child is This?" (_Christmas Carols New and Old_, No. XIV), as printed.** E minor, 6/8, an eighth-note pickup, the 8-bar verse with the chorus left out. Stainer's soprano line has the raised 6th (C sharp) only at the cadence and the natural C in bars 1 and 5, and bar 4 drops to the low B for one beat. _Rejected:_ including the chorus (it would double the length and repeat the verse's material).
- **2026-10-07 — The 6/8 staff gets a contained beaming fix in `src/staff/abc.js`.** The ABC writer beamed only notes shorter than a beat, and in 6/8 the beat is an eighth, so every eighth got its own flag. Now flagged notes (shorter than a quarter) are beamed within a beam group: one beat in simple meter, three eighths in compound meter (3/8, 6/8, 9/8, 12/8), counted from the first downbeat. The ABC for Ode to Joy and St. James is unchanged; two tests in `tests/staff/abc.test.js` pin the 6/8 behavior. _Rejected:_ writing explicit beam breaks per tune; choosing a 3/4 minor tune to avoid 6/8.
- **2026-10-07 — Blurbs keep the existing rule: nothing about key or mode.** The landing's cards are in front of the key question, so "minor" would give the answer away. Each blurb names the meter feel and the pickup instead. _Rejected:_ the brief's example style ("3/4, a pickup, …") verbatim where it named a mode.
- **2026-10-07 — Tunes stay out of C major.** Every demo starts on the provisional C, so a tune in C makes "what key is this?" a non-question (the reason Ode to Joy stays in D). Both editions are already out of C. _Rejected:_ Twinkle in Mozart's C (K. 265) without transposing it.
- **2026-10-07 — Jingle Bells was encoded and then dropped.** It was a fallback for When the Saints while no early print of the familiar Saints tune turned up. Its chorus (Chapple's _Heart Songs_, 1909) put C minor ahead of its true A-flat major in `rankKeys`, because the chorus hammers on degree 3. Matthew chose the final set. _Rejected:_ shipping it alongside.
- **2026-10-07 — Oh! Susanna is not used.** A clean 1909 print exists (_Heart Songs_, p. 172), but it is a minstrel song and that printing's second verse carries a racial slur. We never show lyrics, but the citation points at them. _Rejected:_ citing them anyway for the I–IV–V workhorse slot.
- **2026-10-07 — A test covers every file in `content/songs/`** (`tests/content/songs.test.js`): it validates against the store's invariants, checks the file name matches the id and the bars sit on the meter grid, checks that `rankKeys` puts the encoded key in the top two, and checks that the tune is registered in `src/finding/demoTunes.js`. The content validator already covers every file in the folder. _Rejected:_ hard-coding each new tune into the existing per-tune tests.
- **2026-10-07 — When the Saints Go Marching In is the traditional melody as commonly sung, with no printed source (Matthew's call, Oct 7).** No pre-1931 print carries the familiar tune. Black and Purvis (1896) is a different tune. In R. E. Winsett's 1927 gospel arrangement (_Voice of Glory_ No. 148, reprinted in _Soul Inspiring Songs_, 1929, No. 183) the refrain's words are only a bass echo, under a soprano line that isn't the familiar tune. Matthew made an explicit exception: the refrain is encoded in F major (out of C, so the key question stays a question), 4/4, with a three-beat pickup and 15 bars, cross-checked note for note against the Wikimedia Commons MIDI "When The Saints.mid" (in G, transposed). It is first in the ear-check sheet. _Rejected:_ Winsett's 1927 soprano line as printed (not the tune people know); dropping Saints; Jingle Bells as the I–IV–V stand-in.
- **2026-10-07 — The golden-path e2e gets a 60 s timeout.** It ran in 29.5 s alone against Playwright's 30 s default, and timed out once under a loaded machine with six workers. The test does a lot (finder, chips, dropdown, song switch, tutor), so it gets `test.setTimeout(60_000)` (the coordinator's ask). _Rejected:_ raising the global timeout; splitting the test.
