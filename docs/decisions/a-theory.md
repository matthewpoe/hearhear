# Stream A: theory core decisions

Format: **date — decision.** Why. _Rejected:_ alternatives. Every exported name and shape in `src/theory/index.js` is unchanged; the deviations below are in behavior the contract left open, or where the PRD was silent.

## Structure

- **2026-10-07 — One file per concept, re-exported from `index.js`.** `pitch.js` (spelling, degrees, the home octave), `keyboard.js` (number-row mapping), `numerals.js` (Roman, Nashville, letters), `harmonicFunction.js` (`functionOf`, the only reader of `functions.json`), `harmony.js` (`chordTones`, `analyzeNoteOverChord`), `candidates.js`, `fit.js`, `keyFinding.js` (`rankKeys`), `rhythm.js` (`guessRhythm`), `voicing.js` (`voice`), `meter.js` (now also `rebar`), and `keyChange.js` (`transposeSong`, `rekeySong`). _Rejected:_ one 450-line module (the Phase 0 stub); a folder per concept (ceremony for files this size).

## Pitch and the number row

- **2026-10-07 — The home octave starts at the tonic at or above middle C (C4 to B4).** **Changes the stub's register** (G3 to F#4). The three note rows plus 8/9/0 span 40 semitones and the keyboard spans 48 (C2 to C6), so no rule fits every key. Putting 1 at or above middle C keeps the A row, two octaves down, at C2 or above in every key; only the 0 key in A major, Bb major, and Bb minor, and 9 and 0 in B major and B minor, reach past C6, by at most three semitones. Both demo songs read naturally: Ode's opening F#4 is 3, St. James's B4 pickup is 5, and its high E is 1 on the 8 key. _Rejected:_ the stub's G3 to F#4 (G, A, Bb, and B keys put the A row as low as G1, below the samples); a per-song octave (breaks "the same key always plays the same note").
- **2026-10-07 — Chromatic spelling follows the number row's modifiers.** In major, notes outside the scale are b2, b3, #4, b6, b7 (the blues and borrowed-chord spellings); in minor, b2, #4, and the raised 3rd, 6th, and 7th (so Shift+7 is D# in E minor, the leading tone). When the preferred name would need a double accidental, the enharmonic twin is used (G natural, not Abb, in Gb major). `spell` and `midiToDegree` share this one table, so the staff, the snapshot, and the degree labels always agree. Consequence: Shift+1 in C plays C#, which `midiToDegree` names b2 (Db); the pitch is right, the label follows the key's convention, not the keystroke. _Rejected:_ sharps up and flats down (needs melodic context the API doesn't take); flats-in-flat-keys, sharps-otherwise (the stub; spells D major's b6 as A#, which reads as #5).
- **2026-10-07 — `keyEventToDegree`: Shift and Alt together cancel to a natural.** The PRD doesn't say; cancelling is the least surprising outcome of an accidental chord of modifiers.

## Numerals and function

- **2026-10-07 — `numeralOf` names a dominant seventh as applied (V7/x) when it resolves down a fifth to a diatonic chord other than the tonic or a diminished chord.** D7 in D is V7/IV, E7 is V7/V, B7 in E minor stays V7. Only the `7` type is detected: a major triad on II stays "II", because a major triad off the scale is just as often borrowed (IV in minor, the Picardy I) as it is a secondary dominant, and calling the Dorian IV in E minor "V/VII" would mislead. In minor, the degree-5 target is written V (V7/V), matching the dropdown. Round-trips through `chordFromNumeral` for every chord type and every dropdown chord. _Rejected:_ detecting triads too (above); vii°7/x detection (#iv°7 already reads correctly and is colored dominant by the raised-root rule).
- **2026-10-07 — Nashville stays plain for secondary dominants** (D7 in D is "17", B7 is "67"), per the PRD. Only the Roman numeral is applied.
- **2026-10-07 — `functionOf` is unchanged from Phase 0's implementation**, now in its own file. Consequence worth knowing: borrowed chords the table doesn't list (bIII in major; I and IV in minor) come back "other", gray with a "?" chip. That follows `functions.json` and the PRD ("anything unlisted is neutral gray... invites asking Claude"). Changing it is a contract change, not made.

## Note over chord, candidates, fit

- **2026-10-07 — `analyzeNoteOverChord`: a clash is a note a semitone above a chord tone; every other non-chord tone is a tension.** That catches the classic avoid notes (the 4th over a major third, the b9 over a root, the b6 over a fifth, the major 3rd over a minor chord, the major 7th over a dominant seventh) and leaves 9ths, 6ths, #11, major 7ths over triads, and blue notes (b3 over a major chord, b5 over a minor one) as tensions, which matters for the blues material. A dominant seventh also takes its altered b9 and b13 as tensions. _Rejected:_ the stub's "only a minor 2nd above the root clashes" (misses the 4th over a major third, the commonest clash in folk melody); a full jazz avoid-note table per chord-scale (more rules than the alpha can explain in a why label).
- **2026-10-07 — Roles for chord types the `NoteRole` enum doesn't name.** A sus chord's 2nd or 4th is reported as "third" (it stands in for the third), and a sixth chord's 6th as "seventh" (the added tone). `interval` carries the exact name ("4P", "6M") so a why label can say more. For chord tones `interval` is the chord's own interval ("5d" in a diminished chord); for non-chord tones it is the semitone name. _Rejected:_ adding roles (a contract change).
- **2026-10-07 — Extended candidates.** Major adds V7/ii, V7/iii, V7/IV, V7/V, V7/vi; borrowed iv, bIII, bVI, bVII; and passing diminished sevenths #i°7, #ii°7, #iv°7, #v°7. Minor adds V7/III, V7/iv, V7/V, V7/VI; borrowed I and IV from the parallel major; and #iv°7 and #vii°7. Secondary dominants are sevenths, so every one reads back as an applied numeral. _Rejected:_ VII in minor's extended list (it is diatonic, not one of the three PRD categories, and the PRD's minor list leaves it out); the Neapolitan (rare in the alpha's idioms).
- **2026-10-07 — `fit`'s span and weights.** The span runs from the anchor note to the next placed chord, or else to the end of the anchor's bar (or of the anchor note, if it is held across the bar line). Each melody note starting in the span weighs its sounding duration times its beat strength: downbeat 1, mid-bar strong beat 0.75 (beat 3 of 4/4, beat 4 of 6/8), other beats 0.5, offbeats 0.25. Roles score root, third, and fifth 1, seventh 0.75, tension 0.4, clash 0. The fifth scores the same as the root and third: an earlier draft scored it 0.9, which let iii outrank I under Ode's opening bar. Ties are left to the dropdown's stable sort, which keeps the PRD's more common chord first. _Rejected:_ spans to the next chord with no bar cap (with no chords placed, the first chord would be scored against the whole song).

## Key finding, rhythm, voicing

- **2026-10-07 — `rankKeys` uses the Krumhansl-Kessler profiles over a duration-weighted pitch-class histogram, and names keys by the PRD's conventional list** (F# major and Eb minor where it offers a choice). Scores are Pearson correlations; with no notes, every key scores 0. `outOfScale` uses natural minor plus the raised 6th and 7th, so a cadential leading tone isn't flagged. Ode puts D major first; St. James puts E minor first, ahead of G major.
- **2026-10-07 — `guessRhythm` groups gaps within 20% of each other** to find the most common one, since human timing never repeats a gap exactly, and takes the group's mean as the beat; the earliest group wins a tie. Everything else is the PRD's algorithm as written. Two choices the PRD leaves open: a note followed by a rest keeps its held length snapped to the grid, leaving at least half a beat of rest; and with fewer than two notes there is no gap to measure, so the beat defaults to 625 ms (96 BPM). _Rejected:_ exact-equality modes (never repeat in real playing); a held-length beat for a single note (could set an absurd tempo).
- **2026-10-07 — `voice` places every inversion in close position with its top note in the octave just under `below`, then picks the one that moves least from `previous`** (summed nearest-note distance both ways, so a triad and a seventh chord compare). With no previous chord it picks root position. The band is fixed by `below`, so a long passage never drifts and every alternative auditioned at one spot shares a register. _Rejected:_ choosing the first chord by closeness to a reference pitch (root position names the harmony most clearly for a first A/B).

## Transpose and re-key

- **2026-10-07 — `transposeSong` names the new tonic from the PRD's conventional list and moves chord roots by the same spelled interval,** so every numeral and degree is unchanged. Whole octaves (the octave control) keep the tonic's spelling. Where the PRD offers a choice the default is F# major and Eb minor; the user picks the twin by re-keying. Roots may come out with a double flat (Bb's bVI in Db is Bbb), which the song schema allows and which keeps the numeral right. _Rejected:_ simplifying double accidentals (Bbb as A reads as #V, not bVI); a third parameter for the enharmonic choice (a contract change).
- **2026-10-07 — `rekeySong` may respell chord roots, never change their pitch.** Re-keying to an enharmonic twin (F# major to Gb major) respells every root by the same interval, so the numerals stay the same; any other re-key respells only a root the new key can't name (G# in Gb major becomes Ab), which would otherwise show "?". The notes array is untouched. _Rejected:_ leaving roots alone (F# major's I would read #VII in Gb); respelling every root to the key's default spelling (would turn #v°7 into bvi°7 and change its color).
