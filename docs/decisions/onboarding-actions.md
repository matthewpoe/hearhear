# Onboarding: every tip and tour step waits for one action

Format: **date — decision.** Why. _Rejected:_ alternatives.

Matthew: the onboarding "needs to be way more actionable and way more concise ... every callout that appears [should] have a tangible action that the user must take to advance to the next step." Builds on `docs/decisions/k-callouts.md` and `t-tour.md`, and the tour entry in DECISIONS.md (PR 32).

- **2026-10-07 — No Next and no Got it, in the tips or the tour.** A tip is done only when its `doneWhen` fact holds (the existing `actedOn`), and a tour step only when its `done` condition holds. `noNext` is gone because every tip is now that way. `tests/onboarding/actions.test.js` checks that every tip has a `doneWhen` the tour can observe, and that no tip's `when` already requires it. It checks that every step has a `done`, and that neither component's markup has a Next, Got it or Back button. _Rejected:_ keeping Next as a secondary "skip" (it is the advance Matthew asked to remove).
- **2026-10-07 — One quiet escape each: "Turn tips off" in the tip, "Leave tour" in the strip.** "Turn tips off" replaces the ×. It turns tips off as the masthead toggle does, and it does not mark the tip done, so turning tips back on shows the same tip. Escape now folds the tip into the "Tip" chip instead of closing the tour for the visit. It doesn't finish the tip either. _Rejected:_ the × closing for the visit while marking the tip seen (a way forward without the action); Escape turning tips off (a stray Escape would change a remembered setting).

- **Why this order.** Each step serves the North Star: think in relationships, not pitches; your ear does the work, and the tool shortens the feedback loop.
  1. **Load:** a tune everyone can hum, with its key hidden, so the ear has a job.
  2. **Listen:** hear it and hum it before anything is labelled; notice where it wants to stop.
  3. **Find home by ear:** the core act. Hold each candidate home underneath and feel which one the tune settles on. The tool makes each guess quick to test, and it doesn't hand over the answer.
  4. **Count from home:** "3 3 4 5" is the tune as relationships to home, not pitches. It's the first payoff of finding home.
  5. **A question at bar 4:** V under the long note shows what a chord does relative to home: it asks.
  6. **Now let it land:** vi swerves and I answers. Hearing that difference is what the ear is learning.
  7. **Ask why:** the tutor explains what the ear just felt, after the ear has done the work.
  8. **Another key:** new sound, same numbers. It proves the relationships, not the pitches, were the thing.

  Then the send-off: the same tools on a tune of your own.
